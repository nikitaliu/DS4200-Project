Financial framing

The dashboard treats listings as an investable market. We estimate cap rate and gross rent multiplier proxies, compare median listing price to local purchasing power, and test whether buyers are paying premiums for livability or overlooking environmental exposure.

Key questions

  • Which towns carry the steepest valuation premium relative to local income?
  • How much of price movement is explained by square footage, bathrooms, and livability?
  • Do environmental risks show up as a discount, or are they being absorbed by location desirability?

Data and Methods

Single source of truth: cleaned listings plus ACS income

The pipeline starts from Zillow-style Massachusetts listings, standardizes price, score, and risk fields, extracts prior sale information from listing history, and joins town-level median household income and population from the 2023 ACS 5-year estimates. Every chart on this page is fed from data/ma_housing_cleaned.csv and its town-level derivative.

Primary listing fields

Price, square footage, bedrooms, bathrooms, year built, property type, monthly payment estimate, walk/bike/transit scores, school distances, and five Zillow risk dimensions are preserved in the cleaned file.

Engineered financial metrics

Price per sqft, age of home, livability composite, environmental risk composite, price appreciation, annualized appreciation, estimated cap rate, gross rent multiplier, price-to-income ratio, and monthly affordability index are all computed directly in Python.

Assumption note: Estimated cap rate uses a rough rental proxy: estimatedAnnualRent = estimatedMonthlyPayment × 12 × 1.1. This is clearly labeled as a heuristic, not a direct observed rent series.
